ECM mechanical state × microgroove guidance

This model screens that interaction and helps prioritize groove depths for a first wet-lab comparison; it does not predict a final membrane.

Three short views keep the decision logic, translation path, and evidence boundary separate.

Candidate rule

Read all three metrics

Prioritize depths that improve organization without sacrificing inward migration or matrix infill across matched seeds.

Depth is a screening input, not an optimum. Its strength mapping is phenomenological and rises monotonically by construction. A deep groove may therefore increase modeled alignment while still being a poor experimental candidate.

Published basis

ECM state matters

The ECM-state axis is anchored in gingival-fibroblast-specific evidence from Makkar et al. (2026) and informed by earlier work on mechanical regulation of inflammatory cell state and mechanically tunable cell-instructive matrices.

Testable hypothesis

Topography × ECM

Whether ECM state modifies microgroove-driven alignment, inward migration, or matrix deposition in gingival fibroblasts remains to be tested.

Model output

Prioritization only

The animation ranks comparisons generated by encoded rules. It is not biological evidence or a prediction of healing.

Model boundary. This is a qualitative, uncalibrated prioritization tool. Groove depth is encoded as increasing guidance, while ECM state is operationalized through orientational noise, persistence, and matrix-deposition probability; neither the groove-depth nor the ECM-state mapping is fitted to gingival-fibroblast data. Width, pitch, viscoelasticity, inflammatory signalling, bone-side biology, complete mucosal closure, and post-arrival integration are not modelled. Premature exposure is represented by a fixed linear band—not a finite, remodelling wound—and ECM state refers to the surrounding matrix, not membrane bulk stiffness.

Decision boundary. The ECM-dependence hypothesis would not be supported under the tested conditions if repeated matched-seed runs produced similar groove–flat contrasts in lower- and higher-stiffness ECM. A reproducible, non-zero groove effect of similar magnitude in both states would instead indicate ECM-robust guidance; no reproducible groove effect would leave that depth unsupported for that specific metric.

  2. Vining et al., Nature Materials, 2022: general precedent for mechanics-dependent inflammatory cell state in a monocyte/fibrotic-bone-marrow system, not evidence for gingival fibroblasts. DOI: 10.1038/s41563-022-01293-3.
  3. Vining et al., Biomaterials, 2019: experimental precedent for tuning hydrogel mechanics; viscoelasticity is not represented in this model. DOI: 10.1016/j.biomaterials.2018.10.013.
  4. Clark et al., Development 108, 635–644 (1990): groove dimensions and contact guidance in non-gingival cell systems (DOI: 10.1242/dev.108.4.635). Lacroix et al., Nature Physics 20, 1324–1331 (2024): conceptual precedent for axis-aligned, bidirectional collective migration in human bronchial epithelial monolayers; not evidence for gingival fibroblasts (DOI: 10.1038/s41567-024-02510-3).
  5. Lo et al., Biophysical Journal 79, 144–152 (2000), and Ji & Huang, Communications Biology 6, 1169 (2023): evidence that ECM stiffness can influence migration, including positive and negative durotaxis; neither study validates the model’s monotonic stiffness–persistence mapping in gingival fibroblasts. DOI: 10.1016/S0006-3495(00)76279-5; DOI: 10.1038/s42003-023-05554-y.
  6. Abercrombie & Heaysman, Experimental Cell Research, 1953 (DOI: 10.1016/0014-4827(53)90098-6); Stramer & Mayor, Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology, 2017 (DOI: 10.1038/nrm.2016.118): contact inhibition of locomotion used to motivate short-range collision behavior.
  7. Baraka et al., BDJ Open 11, 58 (2025): systematic-review evidence on human gingival-fibroblast responses to TiO₂ nanotube surfaces, used as tissue-relevant nanotopography context—not validation of the modeled microgroove depths. DOI: 10.1038/s41405-025-00293-0.
  8. Coeyman et al., Current Opinion in Physiology, 2022: general fibrotic matrix-deposition feedback, used here as a non-gingival proxy. DOI: 10.1016/j.cophys.2022.100560.
